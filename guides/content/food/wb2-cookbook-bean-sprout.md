# Cookbook: Bean Sprout

_Wikibooks textbook pages (Wikibooks, CC BY-SA 4.0), converted to plain Markdown: navigation templates, images, references and link lists were removed._

| Legume

The **bean sprout** is a very young sprouted bean plant, harvested prior to the development of leaves. It is similar to but not the same as microgreens.

## Characteristics

Bean sprouts tend to be white to pale yellow in color, with very small little leaves. They are long and crunchy, with a very high water content. Overall, they have an extremely mild, if ever-so-slightly bitter flavor, and they tend to take on the characteristics of the dish they're included in. Care should be taken not to overcook them, as they become translucent, brown, and limp.

## Varieties

Technically, bean sprouts can be made from any dried, nontoxic pulses; however, certain varieties are more common:

- **Mung bean sprouts:** some of the more popular and commonly available bean sprouts; long and tubular.
- **Soy bean sprouts:** similar in appearance to mung bean sprouts, but larger.
- **Alfalfa sprouts:** thinner and more threadlike, somewhat similar to microgreens; not as thick and "juicy" as the above.

## Production

Bean sprouts are fairly easy to produce at home from dried pulses, since all that is required is germinating the plants and letting them form shoots. They should be soaked for 8–12 hours in cool water, drained, and placed in a perforated container in the dark and at room temperature. Repeat the washing/draining daily until you have sprouts.

## Selection and storage

When purchasing bean sprouts, you should only buy those that are crisp and pale. Discard any sprouts that are limp, slimy, or that smell bad.

Bean sprouts should be eaten fresh, and they have a short shelf life. Once you have mature sprouts, they should be kept dry in an airtight container in the fridge—do not wash them until immediately prior to using them. After washing bean sprouts prior to use, discard any that are not crisp and white, then dry them well before using in cooking.

## Use

Bean sprouts are particularly associated with Chinese cuisine, and with east Asian cuisine in general. Bean sprouts are often added to stir-fry, where they add a crunchy texture. They should be added near the end, because they are very easy to overcook.
