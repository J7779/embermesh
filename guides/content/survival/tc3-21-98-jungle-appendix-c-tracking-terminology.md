# Tracking Terminology

*From TC 3-21.98, Jungle Handbook (US Army, 18 August 2026), Appendix C. Paragraph numbers are the manual's own. Illustrations are not reproduced; their captions are kept so that references to figures still make sense.*

This appendix defines the different terminology.

C-1. Casting: this is a drill used to locate or relocate sign. For example, where a tracker is endeavoring to locate sign at a start point/incident site or locate sign that has been abandoned or lost.

C-2. Cold track/sign: a track which is more than 24-hours old.

C-3. Combat tracking/combat TT: tracking at speed to close with the target to re-establish contact.

C-4. Conclusive: sign classified as being ‘conclusive’ is deemed decisive sign belonging to the target.

C-5. Deception: deception tactics are the attempts made by the enemy to lose/delay the tracker.

C-6. EOT: a term used to describe the four main aspects of tracking, particularly when training trackers.

C-7. Entry and exit points: these are points or passageways through which a single or number of any one quarry may pass from one environment to another.

C-8. Environment: the immediate surroundings or conditions of life or growth. The environment will frequently change, for example, moving from within the jungle, into a stream then back into the jungle, will incorporate an environment of leaves on the floor, to that of sand, then back to leaves.

C-9. Foot roll: the sole of the foot rolling through its full length from the rear of the foot.

C-10. Foul track: is defined as a track which has entered an area where numerous other tracks exist. These may be animal tracks, vehicle tracks, or the track left by another target group.

C-11. Found/located: information gained through searching, for example the information identified at an incident site.

C-12. Gait: the individual’s manner of walking. Identifying the gait of an enemy will assist a tracker in identifying a sign pattern amongst his quarries.

C-13. Hot debrief: an immediate debrief on completion of a tracking task given to pass on timely and accurate tactical information to commanders.

C-14. Hot track/sign: a track which is less than 2-hours old.

C-15. Incident: an incident/incident site is an area where the sign indicates to the tracker that the enemy has stopped and carried out some form of action. Examples include an enemy tactical pause, tactical halt or overnight halt.

C-16. Inconclusive: sign that is classified as being ‘inconclusive’ is sign that cannot be confirmed as belonging to the target.

C-17. Indicator pace: this is the pace immediately before or after a change of direction. An indicator pace is normally a shorter pace.

C-18. Key sign: this is that sign which is prominent in any one environment within a track and serves to increase the range and speed of tracking, for example, the sign that stands out the most to the tracker, for example in primary jungle-color change (overturned leaves).

C-19. Last definite sign: the last definite sign is the last point at which the tracker can identify enemy sign. While tracking the tracker should never pass his last definite sign. An exception would be when the tracker is going to probe openings. Under such circumstances the last definite sign is marked before the tracker moves beyond it.

C-20. Opening: an entrance into the undergrowth where the target may have moved through.

C-21. Pace tracking: this is ‘pace for pace’ tracking carried out during the ‘tuning-in’ period.

C-22. Patrol report: the report form used to record the information gained from a tracking patrol task.

C-23. Patrol signals report: signals message sent at the end of each day.

C-24. Pointers: these are those signs that indicate the direction of movement, for example, low foliage (grass) when tramped on or brushed forward, will remain in the direction of movement until disturbed again.

C-25. Primary impact point: the first part of the foot to strike the ground.

C-26. Probe: to investigate an area/opening when looking for further sign.

C-27. Quarry/target: persons who are being followed or pursued.

C-28. Sign pattern: this is the sign that serves to indicate the habits or the peculiarities of an individual or group. For example, the enemy patrol may have an individual who continually snaps twigs on route.

C-29. Sign/spoor: the marks inflicted by a target on the environment. For example, snapped twigs, bruised roots, and so forth. In operational tracking terminology sign is left by humans, Spoor is an Afrikaans word sometimes used when following sign. For example, ‘the TT was following the spoor…’ Sign can be defined as: ‘Any evidence of change from the natural state that is inflicted upon the environment by the passage of man, animal or machinery’.

C-30. Straight edge: this is the line found on leaves or blades of grass caused by the application of pressure, as in the folding of paper.

C-31. TP: the last part of the foot to leave the ground before it makes another step.

C-32. Time bracket: all sign in the initial stages is placed into a time bracket, for example, the time lapse between the earliest possible time that the sign could have been made and the time it was located, in the bracket, for example, the age of sign.

C-33. To follow: proceeding after the enemy without actively seeking to catch them. Trackers follow the enemy while employed on information reporting tasks.

C-34. To pursue/in pursuit: actively following the enemy to close with them to establish contact.

C-35. Track: the path of the target identified by the sign left.

C-36. Track isolation: this is where the tracker having anticipated the intended route of the target is able to ‘leapfrog’ ahead and through the sign pattern of his target is able to identify the same track.

C-37. Track picture: this is the overall picture (summary of findings) gained by the tracker over any given length of track.

C-38. Tracking presentation: a tracking presentation is the term used to describe a formal presentation by a tracker following a patrol on the information gained from a track. It is an instructional tool used normally during training; however, it can be used for a specific purpose during operations.

C-39. Track pursuit drill: the procedure employed while visual tracking.

C-40. Track trap: an area that is conducive to leaving and retaining good sign and spoor.

C-41. TT: this is the British Army’s fundamental tracking unit.

C-42. Tuning-in: this is the initial reading of the sign that enables the tracker to think and act as the target being pursued. Each time the tracker identifies a change in environment the tracker should stop and tune-in. The tracker should think what sign is going to be left in this environment, what is likely to be the key sign, and how will the target move in this environment, for example, how will he negotiate obstacles, and so forth. Tuning-in properly will assist the tracker in anticipating the enemy’s future actions.

C-43. Warm track/sign: a track which is between 2- and 24-hours old.

C-44. VT: a trained tracker.
