# Protection in the Nuclear Age (1985), Appendices B–D: Expedient Fallout Shelter Plans

> **Editor's note:** This is FEMA's 1985 edition of Protection in the Nuclear Age (H-20), reproduced as a historical civil-defence document. Its physics (fallout decay by the seven-ten rule, distance-mass-time protection, shielding equivalents) remains valid, and its core advice matches current US guidance for a nuclear detonation: 'Get inside, stay inside, stay tuned' — shelter in the most shielded space for at least 24 hours and follow official instructions. Some details are dated: radiation is now measured in sieverts/rem rather than roentgens, the Emergency Broadcast System has become the Emergency Alert System and Wireless Emergency Alerts, siren conventions vary by community, public fallout-shelter signage and free shelter plans are no longer maintained, and modern guidance does not recommend tincture of iodine or 'carbon tetrachloride' extinguishers. Use current first-aid and water-treatment guides where they differ.

## Appendix B: Expedient Fallout Shelter – Above-Ground Door-Covered Shelter

### General Information

The above-ground door-covered shelter is designed for areas where below-ground shelters are impractical because the groundwater table or bedrock is close to the ground surface. This shelter can be built by four persons working a total of 10 hours each.

Read and study all instructions before starting to build. If door widths measure less than 32 inches, use a combination of doors to provide a minimum of 32 inches of door-width per person.

### Step 1

Select a shelter location where there is little or no chance of rainwater ponding on the ground surface. Stake out shelter, remove door knobs. Allow 1 door for each person plus 1 door for entry/exit at end. Limit is 8 persons per shelter.

*(Drawing in the original: doors (32 inches wide), one per person plus one for exit/entry, stand on edge in two parallel rows to form the side forms of a long box, held apart by temporary braces 3 feet long; earth-filled rolls are stacked against the outside of the door forms; the entry is offset 22 inches at one end.)*

### Step 2

Set up doors as forms around which earth-filled rolls will be placed. Nail only top braces. Nails must be removed later. Brace all corners, center, top and bottom of each door.

### Step 3

Begin to place earth-filled rolls against door forms, to form earth rolls. See earth-filled roll detail below.

### Earth-Filled Roll Detail

1. Place 2 ft of sheet on ground and temporarily drape remainder of sheet on door.
2. Place earth on sheet – shape as shown (a low roll against the foot of the door).
3. Fold sheet over shaped earth.
4. Place earth onto sheet at narrow trench.
5. Fold sheet to form earth hook. Hook will anchor sheet.
6. Repeat to form next earth-filled roll.

### Step 4

Dig 14-inch deep, 36-inch wide trench inside shelter. Earth can be used to form side earth-filled rolls. Trench can be made up to 3 feet deep if conditions permit.

### Step 5

Mound earth against the earth-filled rolls as shown. Continue placing earth and sheets to form earth-filled rolls.

### Step 6

Keep height of earth about equal on both sidewalls as rolls are formed. After sidewalls have reached planned height, remove braces and door forms. Use same door forms to construct endwalls with earth-filled rolls. Provide exit/entry at end as shown.

### Step 7

Remove door forms from endwalls. Position roof doors in their final position. Place entry frame for door over entry/exit. Place waterproofing material on doors. Fold waterproofing material under the higher edge of the door to keep it from slipping.

*(Cross-section in the original: the roof doors span about 36 inches between the earth-roll walls, which are about 20 inches high on one side and 24 inches on the other so that the roof slopes to drain; the floor trench below is 14 inches or more deep; the whole structure is mounded over with earth. Entry/exit frame: make it of 2 x 4 inch boards, about 22 inches wide to fit the entry, with side supports and a bottom brace, sized to fit the entryway and installed after the entry is completed.)*

### Step 8

Place 15 inches of earth on top of shelter. In hot weather construct a shelter ventilation air pump.

**Note:** If trenching is impractical, heighten walls by using additional earth-filled rolls.

*(Plan view in the original, 4-person shelter: a trench 36 inches wide and about 13 feet long under a roof of doors, earth-filled roll end-walls, an entry frame supporting a door over the entryway, pillowcase sandbags at the entryway to improve radiation shielding, and a 4- to 6-inch diameter pipe through the mounded earth at the far end for ventilation.)*

### Tools and Materials

1. Doors as indicated.
2. Pick or mattock and shovel.
3. Two buckets or large cans to carry earth.
4. Tape measure, yardstick or T ruler.
5. Saw, axe or hatchet.
6. Hammer and at least 20 nails – 2½ inches long.
7. At least 4 double bed sheets for each person to be sheltered.
8. Pillowcases and rainproofing materials such as plastic or polyethylene.
9. Work gloves for each worker.
10. Lumber for use as temporary braces and for entry/exit frame.

## Appendix C: Expedient Fallout Shelter – Door-Covered Trench Shelter

### General Information

This shelter is designed for areas where there is a shortage of small trees and/or building materials. The depth to ground water and rock must also be below the bottom of the trench. In addition, the earth must be sufficiently firm and stable so that the trench walls will not collapse. The shelter (3-person capacity) can be constructed by 3 people working an approximate total of 12 hours each. Read and study all instructions before beginning to build.

### Step 1

Select a reasonably level site. Lay out the shelter as illustrated by laying doors side by side to determine the shelter length. Door knobs should be removed.

*(Layout in the original, 3-person capacity: a main trench 7 ft 4 in long and 36 inches wide covered by three 32-inch doors (about 8 ft 0 in in total); a 20- to 24-inch entrance at one end; at the other end a ventilation trench 9 inches deep, 12 inches wide, made about 36 inches long (or 12 inches if no boards or sticks are available) and covered with boards or sticks; earth rolls ("R") or sandbags ("S") placed on all sides of each opening.)*

### Step 2

Excavate the shelter trench, entryway and ventilation trench as shown. Pile the excavated earth at least 3 feet beyond the trench limits so that it will not interfere with the later placement of doors over the trench.

*(Pictorial view in the original, with the earth mound removed: the main trench is about 4 ft 6 in deep; the entrance has steps (about 18 inches and 6 inches) down into the trench; a rainproof "buried roof" lies within the mounded earth above the doors; open-sided canopies on poles about 2 ft high keep fallout and rain out of the entrance and the ventilation trench.)*

### Step 3

If there are adequate sheets or fabric available, line the trench walls with them. Then place doors over the trench.

*(Section A–A in the original: trench 36 inches wide and 4 ft 6 in deep with a lining sheet; doors laid across the top (a second layer if hollow-core doors are used); earth mounded to about 12 inches over the doors, about 10 ft wide, with a rainproof "buried roof" inside the mound and a drainage ditch on all sides. Section B–B: if time and materials are available after completing the shelter, make the shielding around the entryway 12 inches higher; raise the open-sided canopy if the shielding is raised; slope the floor slightly toward the entry.)*

### Step 4

In order to hold in place an adequate amount of earth on top of the doors, construct earth "rolls" around the entryway as shown. The "rolls" will keep the earth fill in place. See How to Make an Earth Roll.

### Step 5

Place earth fill and the waterproofing material over the doors. Place sandbags as shown on the illustrations.

### Step 6

Construct shallow drainage ditches on all sides and place canopies over the openings.

### Tools and Materials

1. Doors (interior solid or hollow-core) – 1 full size (32 inch minimum width) for each person. If doors measure less than 32 inches in width, use a combination of doors to provide the minimum width per person. If doors are hollow core, use two layers.
2. Pick and/or mattock.
3. Long-handled shovels and square-bladed shovel.
4. Rainproofing material (e.g., plastic sheeting, canvas, plastic table covers, etc.) at least 25 square feet per person plus 2 pieces about 6 ft by 6 ft for use as canopies.
5. One bedsheet or the equivalent of 50 sq. ft. of cloth or plastic per person to line trench and make earth-filled rolls.
6. Two pillowcases per person to use as sandbags.
7. String or cord to tie canopies and sandbags.
8. Knife.
9. Several boards about 3 feet long.
10. Measuring tape and/or ruler.
11. Work gloves for each worker.
12. Hammer and hand saw.

### How to Make an Earth Roll

1. Select a piece of cloth or plastic at least as strong as a new bed sheet, 2 ft wider than the side of the opening to be protected, and 5 ft in length.
2. Place 2 ft of the length of the cloth on the ground, as illustrated.
3. While using both hands to hold up 3 ft of the length of the cloth and while pressing against the cloth with your body, have another person shovel earth onto and against the cloth (to a height of about 8 inches).
4. While still pulling on the cloth, place the upper part over the earth that is on the lower part of the cloth.
5. Cover the upper edge of the cloth, forming an earth-filled "hook" in this edge.

## Appendix D: Expedient Fallout Shelter – Log-Covered Trench Shelter

### General Information

This shelter is designed for areas where the depth below the ground surface to hard rock or groundwater is below the bottom of the trench. Also, the earth must be sufficiently firm and stable so that the trench sidewalls will not cave in. In addition, adequate small trees that can be cut for logs must be available in the immediate area. The shelter (4-person capacity) can be built by 4 people working a total of 12 hours each. After initial completion, the shelter can be enlarged to a width of 5 ft 6 in and deepened to 6 ft. However, 9-ft logs must be used in place of 7-ft logs and the buried roof must be large enough to cover the widened shelter during the initial construction.

### Step 1

Clear area of brush and tall grass. Lay out shelter as shown below.

*(Layout in the original, marked with wood or metal stakes, in a straight line: (1) an entrance step 1 ft long, 22 inches wide and 2 ft 6 in deep; (2) an entrance trench 5 ft long, 22 inches wide and 4 ft 0 in deep; (3) the shelter room, 11 ft 0 in long for a family of four (add 2 ft 9 in extra length for each additional occupant), 42 inches wide and 4 ft 6 in deep, with the occupants sitting on one side; (4) a ventilation trench 5 ft long, 22 inches wide and 24 inches deep at the far end.)*

### Step 2

Begin excavating the trench. Place excavated earth at least 3 feet beyond the edge of trench so that the roof logs can later be placed over the trench.

### Step 3

As the trench excavation progresses, workers should begin cutting logs to the length and size as shown on the illustrations.

*(Plan view of the top of the shelter in the original: roof logs at least 7 ft long and at least 4 inches in diameter at the small end, laid side by side about 2 ft past each end of the shelter room and placed about 36 inches beyond the room; alternate big and small ends of logs to keep them straight across the shelter room; entry-trench roof logs at least 5 ft long, small-end diameter at least 3 inches, leaving an entry hole; a ventilation hole and emergency exit 24 inches long by 22 inches wide at the far end; 6-inch minimum diameter logs (or 6-inch-high "sandbags"), notched and toe-nailed, laid along the edges to keep rainwater on the ground out; cover the logs with cloth, plastic or leaves to keep earth out of the shelter.)*

### Step 4

Place logs over trench. Position ties for bed sheet chairs or hammocks. Place newspaper or other material as indicated over logs. Place earth fill and buried roof.

*(Section A–A in the original: room 42 inches wide and 4 ft 6 in deep; roof logs (minimum 7 ft long, 4 inch minimum diameter at the small end) rest on the ground at least 9 inches back from each side of the trench; about 18 inches of earth fill over the logs, then a rainproof "buried roof" of tar paper or plastic film, then more earth to a total of about 36 inches at the centre, smoothed and mounded, with a rainwater ditch around it. If available, place boards 6 to 12 inches wide under the ends of the roof logs as mudsills, or lay sticks about 1 inch in diameter side by side for the outermost 12 inches of the logs to rest on. If boards or sticks are not available to make mudsills under the ends of the logs to take pressure off the edges of the trench, remove the top 1 inch of earth within 9 inches of the side of the trench.)*

### Step 5

Construct canopies over the openings.

*(Pictorial view in the original, with part of the roof cut away to show the buried roof: earth smoothed, mounded and at least 4 inches deep over the ends of the logs before placing the buried-roof rainproofing; cloth, paper, sticks, etc. placed over the logs to keep earth from falling through cracks; 5-ft logs over the entryway and exit trenches; a 6 ft x 6 ft piece of light fabric or plastic as a canopy suspended over the entry, tied to canopy poles 2 ft high with cord pulled tight around sloping stakes; screen or insect netting over both openings to keep out mosquitoes and flies; the 22 x 24 inch ventilation opening and emergency exit covered with a 6 ft x 3 ft piece of light fabric or plastic; two logs 7 ft long held in place by wire or rope on each end act as a retaining wall for earth fill at each end; 6-inch diameter logs with earth packed around them, or sandbags, keep rainwater out.)*

### Tools and Materials

1. Saw and/or axe.
2. Pick or mattock.
3. Long-handled shovels.
4. Rainproofing material (plastic or polyethylene) 25 square yards. For each person above 4, add 2 sq. yds.
5. 50 feet of strong string or cord and a knife.
6. Tape measure or yard stick.
7. At least 8 pillow cases and/or sandbags.
8. Work gloves.
9. Bed sheets for use as "chairs" or "hammocks" – 1 per person at least 15 feet of strong rope or cord per bed sheet.
10. 15 pounds of newspapers to place over roof logs to keep earth from falling through cracks between logs.
