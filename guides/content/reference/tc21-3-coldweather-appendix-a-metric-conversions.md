# US Customary to Metric Conversions (Cold-Weather Handbook)

*From TC 21-3, Soldier's Handbook for Individual Operations and Survival in Cold-Weather Areas (US Army, 30 September 2025), Appendix A. Paragraph numbers are the manual's own. Illustrations are not reproduced; their captions are kept so that references to figures still make sense.*

A-1. The standard modern form of the metric system is officially called the International System of Units. In 1988, Congress designated this system (commonly known as the metric system) as the preferred classification of weights and measurements for trade and commerce. Most of the world operates on the meter for length and the kilogram for weight.

A-2. There are two options for converting U.S. customary system measurements to the metric system: apply mathematics to calculate the answer or consult an online conversion calculator. The math requires only a simple multiplication problem to turn a customary size to a metric size. Table A-1 lists approximate common equivalents between the two systems of measurement. These numbers have been rounded to the second decimal place.

A-3. Table A-1 on page 108 is presented as a handy field reference. There are far more measurement conversion units used in the world today. A comprehensive list of these equivalents can be found at the National Institute of Standards and Technology website. (The website address can be found in the reference section of this publication.)
