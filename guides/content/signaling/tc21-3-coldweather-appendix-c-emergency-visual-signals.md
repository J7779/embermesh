# Emergency Visual Signals in Cold Regions

*From TC 21-3, Soldier's Handbook for Individual Operations and Survival in Cold-Weather Areas (US Army, 30 September 2025), Appendix C. Paragraph numbers are the manual's own. Illustrations are not reproduced; their captions are kept so that references to figures still make sense.*

C-1. Efficient Army field operations depend on clear, concise, and accurate communication. There are many ways that Soldiers can signal with each other using gestures, sounds, and symbols.

C-2. Ground-to-air visual markings that relay specific concepts or requirements can be made with VS-17 panel markers, dragging brush into the desired shape(s), or stomping the symbol into the snow. (See figure C-1.)

*[Figure C-1. Ground-to-air markings — illustration not reproduced.]*

C-3. Perhaps the most common forms of Army signaling are known as hand-and-arm and ground-to-air signals. These tend to be specialty-specific, but many are also used within several disciplines

C-4. Signals that Soldiers are most likely to use in cold-weather situations are depicted in the following figures. Figure C-2 portrays hand-and-arm signals.
